# Sovereignty Stays with People

**AI serves. It does not govern.**

*Airokrasi* combines "AI" with the Greek root *-kratos* (rule, power), the same way *democracy* does. What it means is this: **sovereignty belongs to people, and AI serves.** The name can also be misread as "the power of AI." We reject that reading, and this text exists in part to reject it.

To govern is to decide what is valuable, who gets what, and who bears the cost. Those decisions belong to people and stay with people. AI reads, prepares, calculates and carries out, and it writes down the reasons for every step it takes. Its authority comes from people, covers a defined scope for a defined time, and can be withdrawn through procedures written in advance.

> **General rationale**
> - **Purpose:** To say in one document why Airokrasi is proposed and which values it is bound to. The constitution will be written to follow this document (K18).
> - **Reason:** AI is entering public administration before anyone has written the rules for it, and democracy is tiring at the same time. Together these call for a new way to protect human sovereignty.
> - **Debate summary:** The text was prepared through a structured debate among AI agents. The author wrote the draft; the critic raised two serious objections: the evidence is narrow while the design is broad, and the text does not say where it comes from. This version answers both. It rests on the referee decisions of the Foundations and Economy–Corruption debates. On 2026-11-09 a value referee, who had taken no part in the debates, decided the status and language rules of this text (V-13), moved the public-employment commitment from Part II to Part I (V-14), added a short environment article (V-15), and settled the integrity body, the value share and laws aimed at named persons (V-01 to V-03), which Part I and Article 17 now reflect.

## Part I — Why?

### A letter

A parent opens a letter. The state wants back the childcare benefit she has received for years, and it does not say why. She is wrongly accused of fraud, and proving her innocence takes years. In the Netherlands this happened to about 26,000 parents.

Now imagine the same letter under the rules we propose. The letter states the decision and the reason in plain language. Her subsistence payment normally continues until a person has reviewed the case. She knows whom to call. This is a design goal, not a result: no country has tried it yet.

### We want to protect democracy

Democracy is one of humanity's strongest ways of correcting its own mistakes. It removes bad rulers without bloodshed. It gives the loser hope for next time. The press, the courts and parliament put errors in front of everyone. The automated-decision scandals in the Netherlands and Australia came to light this way, but late, and only after the poor had paid the price.

Yet democracy is tiring. According to Freedom House, global freedom has declined for the twentieth year in a row. In the OECD's 2024 survey, more people had low or no trust in their national government (44%) than had high or moderate trust (39%). Democracies rarely collapse in a coup these days; elected leaders take over institutions from the inside. We think most of these problems are less about speed than about how information and power are distributed.

None of this proves that Airokrasi is good. It sets a design constraint: we must build a system in which the winner does not take all.

### Why now?

**AI is already entering government.** In the Netherlands, a risk-scoring system wrongly accused parents of fraud, and the government resigned. In Australia, an automated debt system issued about 470,000 unlawful debts. In 2025, Albania presented an AI system in charge of public procurement as a "minister." The question is not whether AI will enter government but under what rules. The pattern of failure is clear: the victims were mostly poor, no reasons were given, the burden of proof fell on the citizen, and the correction came years later, from the press and the courts outside the system.

**Persuasion machines are coming.** In controlled experiments, conversational AI shifted political views, and as its persuasiveness increased, its accuracy fell. One study found that between 2006 and 2017, false news spread faster than true news on Twitter. A democracy that cannot protect itself from a machine able to talk to every voter one-on-one has not protected its own foundation.

**Work and income may shift.** We expect unemployment to rise; today's data does not yet show it. What it shows is an early signal: in the occupations most exposed to AI, employment of 22–25-year-olds is about 19% behind that of their peers in less exposed jobs. Causation is not established. So we tie our measures to indicators: we do not spend before it is needed, and we do not wait too long once it is.

### Three roads

The first road is to leave democracy as it is and regulate AI strictly: impact assessments, a right to appeal automated decisions, independent algorithm audits. The second is to do that and also test Airokrasi's institutions. The third is to do nothing and let AI enter public administration through the decisions of vendors and agencies.

We reject the third road. We do not know whether the first is enough or the second is needed. Part of what we propose requires no AI at all: citizens' panels, proportional representation, rules for appointments, conflict-of-interest disclosure, open data. A country could adopt these and leave the rest. What is specific to Airokrasi is this: party models that voters can try out before voting, a sealed election promise, and AI assistants (technical name: AI agents) that draft laws. We want to test these in a shadow period, with the criteria for success and for abandoning them written down in advance.

### What it is not

Airokrasi is not rule by machines: a human judge delivers every binding judgment, a named person stands behind every decision, and "the AI decided" is never an excuse. It is not an AI dictatorship: human rights are the highest norm, and people can stop the system. It is not corporate rule: no single model or vendor may control it. It is not technocracy: it rests on the voters' will and on human judges, not on experts. And it does not promise a flawless machine.

### What it keeps and what it changes

It keeps elections, the separation of powers, rights guarantees, a free press, independent courts and the right to appeal.

It changes this: the preparation and carrying out of decisions passes to AI assistants (AI systems that perform specific tasks). Every decision is published with its reasons and an unalterable audit trail. Parties put before voters not only a program but the AI model that would carry it out. Voters see the answers to common questions, published by an independent body, side by side, and they test the models in face-to-face trials; free-form chat does not bind anyone. The winning party's election promise is sealed: independent bodies verify, and publish, that the model applied is the program you voted for. Most election promises are already kept (a study of more than 20,000 pledges in 12 countries shows this). The reason for the seal is not that promises go unkept but that whether they were kept can be verified. A seal is not a guarantee of behavior: if an error appears, the system is stopped, handed over, renewed.

The human role becomes simpler and heavier: to choose. A machine may offer options; people set the goal, choose, reject, ask again, and within limits written in law say "none of these." This applies to the head of state too. A named person is responsible for whether the options were adequate.

### What we promise

*What any good system should offer* (not specific to Airokrasi, but our commitment):
- Every decision comes with a written, plain-language answer to "why?" The last stop for an appeal is a person.
- Subsistence payments, outside narrow written exceptions, are not cut while a review is under way.
- We do not claim to end corruption in major procurement and zoning. Human discretion remains there, and so does favoritism. AI assistants can make it visible and costly. We claim no more than that.
- An independent integrity body prevents corruption, analyses risk, examines cases and gathers evidence. It does not prosecute and it does not impose sanctions: prosecution stays with prosecutors, and coercive measures such as searches, wiretaps and freezing assets stay with judges. Its powers can be widened only under written conditions (two years in a row of audited equal treatment of all political camps, and prosecution safeguards in place), and only from the next legislative term.
- No law may be aimed at punishing or burdening a named person or group. A provision that favors a named person, firm, parcel or region is flagged, published and open to an equality review, and the burden of proof lies with the legislator.

*What only Airokrasi wants to test:*
- Petty bribery fell markedly in digital-payment and e-government cases (the randomized trial in India, Georgia). We expect the same with AI assistants, but this evidence tested payment infrastructure, not AI assistants.
- We will set rules so that the gains from AI do not pile up in a few hands or institutions, and we will measure and publish the concentration. Citizens will receive a rule-based share (the Citizen's Dividend). We promise no figure.
- No public employee is dismissed because AI assistants take over work. Numbers fall only through voluntary departure and retirement, and never below the floor that keeps human review, appeal and human contact. This is a commitment, not a human right. It is written as a law needing a qualified majority, not into the unamendable core, so that later parliaments can still decide how public services are organized. If it is ever changed or repealed, people in post on that day stay protected unless they are offered an equivalent post, grade and step, retraining and written offers of placement.
- The state may take a share of the value its own decisions create, for example when farmland is rezoned for building. For now this is only calculated and published. Collection could come only as a tax: a fixed band set by a qualified-majority law, no negotiation, applied to realized gains only, collected by the tax authority, and only after a legal opinion and a pilot. We promise no figure.
- Our safeguards may slow some transactions. We promise no speed.

> **Rationale [Part I, promises]**
> - **Purpose:** To state, in the place for commitments and not in the list of universal values, what is specific to Airokrasi, and at what legal rank.
> - **Reason:** Corruption is the founder's biggest concern (K9), but a body that holds data, tips, risk analysis and investigation at once could become a tool of selective enforcement. A law can also write favoritism into a criterion that an agent then applies flawlessly. A commitment on public jobs is not a human right, and making it unamendable would lock in how future parliaments organize the state (K12).
> - **Debate summary:** The value referee chose a narrow integrity body with a written gate for widening (V-01); a value share collected only as a tax, only after preconditions, and for now only calculated and published (V-02); three classes of named-person provisions (V-03); and a qualified-majority law with a guarantee for current employees for the public-employment commitment (V-14).
> - **Source:** V (V-01, V-02, V-03, V-14); E09 (Decisions 11, 19, 27, 34, 37, 45, 48, 51).
> - **Status:** Referee decisions (K31), 2026-11-09. Open to challenge. The legal form of the guarantee for current employees is still a research task, and several outside citations in the decisions are marked as to be verified.

### The biggest risk

The greatest risk of this system is that the winner takes all and the number of people able to object shrinks. Against this: the winner cannot appoint a majority of any oversight board; the system can be stopped by several institutions; human judges and appeal offices always exist. The number of decisions any single responsible authority can take on is limited by law, and anything beyond that limit goes to human approval. If harm arises from the promise itself, political responsibility lies with the party that made it. We do not know whether this is enough.

### What we do not know

- With today's methods, we cannot be certain whether an AI model has a hidden tendency. So we will not say "safe"; we will say "we give this assurance, under these conditions, within these limits."
- There is evidence that human approval can become a formality under pressure. We will measure whether the person who approves really checks.
- Collapse is easy to see; quiet decay is harder. We will watch for it through appeal rates, independent sampling and complaints that come through the human channel.
- Advanced AI models are concentrated in very few institutions today. We do not know whether we can secure model independence in every country. A state run with AI is also a high-value target for attackers.
- Whoever presents the options can steer the decision in practice. We do not know whether our diversity safeguards prevent that enough.
- Keeping records in a ledger makes them harder to alter; it does not guarantee that what they say is true.
- To our knowledge, no country has tried such a system end to end.

So the transition has to be gradual: first a shadow period (AI assistants only make proposals and have no authority), then limited pilots, then scale. At every stage the way back stays open.

## Part II — Our Values

**Status.** This part is a Statement of Values. It is not binding and creates no individual rights of its own. It completes the human rights convention for the questions of the age of AI. It is the source text from which constitutions are written (K18) and a guide to reading them in harmony with these values; a constitution's preamble refers to it.

There are three layers. At the top (K1) stand the human rights treaties and the Airokrasi Declaration of Human Rights (in preparation; it enters this repository as a final text when its debate is complete), which are binding. Below them is this Part II. Part I is not normative: it is reasoning and political commitment.

Rights are written only in the Declaration. The articles below that touch on rights are statements of value, and each points to the Declaration articles it relates to; where the wording differs, the Declaration prevails. If this part conflicts with the convention, the convention prevails: an article is first read in harmony with the convention, and if that is impossible it is treated as not in force and goes back for review. If it conflicts with a national constitution, the constitution stays in force, but the conflict can be found to be a violation and can have consequences for membership. This part holds universal values only; commitments specific to Airokrasi or to the transition are in Part I. The Declaration is a draft under discussion.

### Dignity and rights

**Article 1 — Human dignity.** Every person is born with dignity and equal rights. These rights cannot be given away. No machine, law, vote or state of emergency may permit torture or slavery, or remove the core of any right.

*Declaration:* UDHR Arts. 1, 4, 5, 30.

> **Rationale [Article 1]**
> - **Purpose:** To write down that rights are no one's favor and cannot be removed by a vote or an emergency.
> - **Reason:** In 2009 Switzerland adopted a minaret ban by popular vote (57.51%). A vote alone does not protect rights.
> - **Debate summary:** The legal expert said that absolute rights admit no derogation even in an emergency, and that the unamendable core should be kept short. The referee agreed. The right to withdraw was split in two: institutional withdrawal is free, withdrawal from core rights is not.
> - **Source:** T04 §6; T09 (E1, E3, G4); T03 §2.8.
> - **Status:** The decision it rests on is approved (2026-11-08). The article text is a draft.

**Article 2 — Equality.** No one may be discriminated against, by a person or by an algorithm, because of race, sex, language, belief, origin, disability or political opinion. Those who did not vote for the government receive the same service. No one may be given an overall score for their behavior, and no right or treatment may depend on such a score.

*Declaration:* UDHR Arts. 2, 7; Additional Arts. 6, 7.

> **Rationale [Article 2]**
> - **Purpose:** To ban discrimination, including by algorithms, and to rule out political opinion and behavior scoring as grounds.
> - **Reason:** The Dutch risk-scoring system was criticized for discriminating. A model's political leaning can be steered by fine-tuning.
> - **Debate summary:** The legal expert proposed putting discrimination on political opinion into an additional protocol; the referee added the scope "those who did not vote for the government as well." The ban on an overall score came from the Economy referee's discussion of a service-hours credit.
> - **Source:** T09 (A2, E1); T03 §3.4, §3.6; E09 (Decision 52).
> - **Status:** Foundations decision approved. The "overall score" sentence rests on an Economy decision; there is no separate approval record.

**Article 3 — Thought and information.** Thought, conscience and expression are free; the press is free and a journalist's sources are protected. The state does not label what is true or false; independent organizations do the verifying. No one may be subjected to forged documents, fake sources, or personalized persuasion that targets their weaknesses.

*Declaration:* UDHR Arts. 18, 19; Additional Art. 10.

> **Rationale [Article 3]**
> - **Purpose:** To protect a free information environment, and to draft the ban on manipulation narrowly enough that it cannot become censorship.
> - **Reason:** Freedom of expression is the area autocratizing leaders most often target. The persuasive power of conversational AI has been measured in experiments.
> - **Debate summary:** The first proposal had no press article; the researcher and critic pointed out the gap, and the defender wrote it. The referee limited the ban to measurable facts: opinion, commentary and prediction are outside it. The decision lies with a human judge.
> - **Source:** T03 §1.4, §2.4; T09 (Y2).
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08). The article text is a draft.

**Article 4 — Privacy.** Personal data belongs to the person. The state says what data it holds and why. The right to erasure and correction does not disappear because of any record-keeping technique.

*Declaration:* UDHR Art. 12; Additional Art. 8.

> **Rationale [Article 4]**
> - **Purpose:** To place the right over data above any record-keeping technique.
> - **Reason:** An unalterable ledger can conflict with the right to erasure. The EU data protection board's guidelines strongly discourage writing personal data to a chain and note that even digests can count as personal data.
> - **Debate summary:** The founder wanted a blockchain ledger (K15). The defender and critic proposed a keyed digest; the referee, relying on the guidelines, tightened it: only aggregate root hashes go on the chain, while personal data and individual digests stay in a deletable store.
> - **Source:** T09 (K15); 01b (P2).
> - **Status:** Approved (KB-9, 2026-11-08).

### Humans and machines

**Article 5 — Sovereignty belongs to people.** An AI assistant has no authority of its own. All authority comes from people, covers a defined scope for a defined time, and can be withdrawn through procedures written in advance.

> **Rationale [Article 5]**
> - **Purpose:** To write down where an agent's authority comes from and where it ends.
> - **Reason:** In the age of agents, responsibility can dissolve behind "the system decided." With Albania's "minister" AI, the route of appeal and the person responsible stayed unclear.
> - **Debate summary:** The first principle said "revocable at any moment," which conflicted with a fixed seal for the term. The referee corrected it to "through procedures written in advance."
> - **Source:** `docs/01-ilkeler.md` (İ2); T09 (§8.2); T03 §3.5.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 6 — An AI assistant is a tool.** An AI assistant is not a legal person. Every AI assistant action is legally attributed to a named human authority. "The AI decided" is not an excuse.

*Declaration:* Additional Art. 5.

> **Rationale [Article 6]**
> - **Purpose:** To make sure responsibility always reaches a person.
> - **Reason:** In the Netherlands and Australia, the error disappeared between the system and the institutions.
> - **Debate summary:** The legal expert proposed writing "an agent has no legal personality" into the constitution. The referee asked that responsibility fall not on an "organ" but on a named human authority.
> - **Source:** T04 §5; T09 (§8.2, İ8); T03 §3.4.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 7 — Judgment is human.** Only independent human judges exercise judicial power: the AI assistant prepares, the judge decides. Adopting laws and constitutions, and decisions on emergencies and war, are also human.

*Declaration:* UDHR Arts. 10, 11; Additional Art. 4.

> **Rationale [Article 7]**
> - **Purpose:** To keep binding judgments and the gravest political decisions with people.
> - **Reason:** Judicial independence and the right not to be subject to a purely automated decision are core legal safeguards.
> - **Debate summary:** The founder's phrase "agents run judicial processes" is lawful, in the legal expert's view, provided the binding judgment stays with a human. The founder read "run" as preparation, screening and drafting.
> - **Source:** T04 (A2); T09 (A2); T11 (KB-4).
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 8 — People choose.** An AI assistant may offer options drawn from different sources. To choose, to reject, and, within limits written in law, to say "none of these" or to ask again belongs to people, the head of state included. A named person is responsible for whether the options were adequate.

> **Rationale [Article 8]**
> - **Purpose:** To define the human role as choosing, while limiting the power of whoever produces the options.
> - **Reason:** Whoever defines the options effectively determines the decision (agenda-setting power).
> - **Debate summary:** The critic set four conditions: diversity measured by difference in outcomes, a non-neutral "none of these," a named option-set officer, and an urgent route. The referee accepted all of them. The founder chose the middle way: for appointments, selection from outside the list with a written reason; for other acts, "none of these" and "ask again," limited in number and time.
> - **Source:** T09 (K17, C1); 01b (P4); T11 (KB-12).
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 9 — Reasons and appeal.** Everyone learns, in plain language, the reasons for a decision that affects them, knows whether they are dealing with an AI assistant or a person, and may appeal. The last stop for an appeal is a human judge or a human panel. No one may be judged solely by an automated decision.

*Declaration:* Additional Arts. 1, 2, 3, 4.

> **Rationale [Article 9]**
> - **Purpose:** To guarantee the right to know why a decision was made and to turn to a person.
> - **Reason:** In the Netherlands, parents were given no reasons for the decision; in Robodebt, the burden of proof fell on the citizen; in both cases the citizen was vindicated by the press and courts outside the system.
> - **Debate summary:** The citizen's eye showed that "you can appeal" is not enough (time limits, fees, whom to contact). The Citizen Guarantees was written in response.
> - **Source:** T06; T09 (YGP, E1); T03 §3.4.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 10 — Oversight by risk.** The higher the risk, the larger the human share. In matters touching liberty, family, citizenship, punishment and adverse decisions, the last word is human. Subsistence payments, outside narrow written exceptions, are not cut while a review is under way; the state, not the citizen, pays for its errors.

*Declaration:* Additional Arts. 2, 3, 4.

> **Rationale [Article 10]**
> - **Purpose:** To scale human oversight to the harm a decision can cause.
> - **Reason:** All four algorithmic scandals arose in routine, money-related decisions such as benefits, taxes and grades.
> - **Debate summary:** The critic said "reversibility" was misleading. The referee defined reversibility as "the harm can be made good"; an adverse decision by default requires human approval. The absolute "never cut on any suspicion" was corrected with narrow exceptions for double payment and hijacked accounts.
> - **Source:** T09 (A1, D2, YGP); T11 (KB-8); T03 §3.4.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 11 — Stoppability.** The system can be stopped, in part or in full, through procedures written in advance. The power to stop is not concentrated in one person and cannot be suspended in an emergency. A return to human administration is always kept ready.

> **Rationale [Article 11]**
> - **Purpose:** To keep the last safeguard in case agents slip out of control.
> - **Reason:** This is the safeguard against the biggest risk particular to this model.
> - **Debate summary:** The first principle said "decisions are reversible"; the referee made it "harm can be made good." Stopping needs several keys and a judge's approval. The emergency stop route is independent of the common agent protocol's gateway.
> - **Source:** T04 §6; T09 (§8.2 İ6, K14).
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 12 — Records and openness.** Every decision is open to independent audit with its reasons and an unalterable audit trail (corrected only by adding a further entry). The audit trail carries no personal data. The default is openness; exceptions only by law, proportionate and time-limited.

> **Rationale [Article 12]**
> - **Purpose:** To secure accountability through records.
> - **Reason:** "Full transparency" conflicts with personal data and with the waiting periods for responsible disclosure.
> - **Debate summary:** The legal expert proposed writing "auditability and records" into the core instead of "full transparency." The referee brought in the formula of openness by default with narrow exceptions by law. The ledger is an auditable trail, not evidence in itself; a wrong entry is not deleted but corrected by a further entry.
> - **Source:** T04 §6 (#11); T09 (§8.2 İ4, K15).
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 13 — No single point of dependence.** No critical function may depend on a single model, company, vendor or common communication point. Where that is impossible, the dependency is mapped, published and backed up. An Independent Reviewer (AI) does not rest on the same model or the same developer as the AI assistant it audits.

> **Rationale [Article 13]**
> - **Purpose:** To counter common-mode failure and single-vendor risk.
> - **Reason:** Models that share training data can make the same mistakes; "many models" may be an illusion of diversity. Advanced models are concentrated in very few institutions.
> - **Debate summary:** The security expert said that a safeguard of "5–7 models" might not be enough. The referee wrote the rule per auditor–audited pair, and asked for at least two lineages, with three as the goal, plus a feasibility study. In small countries, finding independent lineages may be hard; this is a known limit.
> - **Source:** T09 (B4, §8.3 İ11, BS); T05; T03 D11.
> - **Status:** Approved (İ11, 2026-11-08).

### Politics and law

**Article 14 — Power changes hands by election.** There are free, equal, secret and regular elections, a plurality of parties, and a right to opposition. Elections cannot be postponed indefinitely. The loser keeps the right to win again. Between elections, too, ways to take part (petitions, citizens' panels chosen by lot, referendums) are guaranteed by law; these do not replace an elected body.

*Declaration:* UDHR Art. 21.

> **Rationale [Article 14]**
> - **Purpose:** To protect peaceful transfers of power and the loser's consent.
> - **Reason:** Democracy stays peaceful because the loser believes they can win later. Assemblies chosen by lot succeeded when tied to elected bodies (Ireland) and lost impact when not (Iceland, France).
> - **Debate summary:** The founder chose proportional representation, a single round, a 3% threshold and a parliamentary executive; a two-round election was rejected. The Citizens' Panel is advisory, under a "follow or explain" rule, not a decision-making body.
> - **Source:** T09 (A3, B6, B8); T11 (KB-7); T03 §1.1, §3.1.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 15 — Separation of powers.** The Drafting Assistant (AI), the Service Assistant (AI) and the Disputes Assistant (AI) are separate; none may instruct another. How oversight bodies are formed is written in the constitution, and no term of government may appoint a majority of any board.

> **Rationale [Article 15]**
> - **Purpose:** To make it harder to capture the boards on which the safeguards depend.
> - **Reason:** Every safeguard leaned on "independent boards," but who appoints the boards was not written down. Elected authoritarianism proceeds exactly this way.
> - **Debate summary:** The critic's most important finding. The Appointments Charter was written: lot and a qualification pool, qualified majorities, mutual elimination and lot in a deadlock. No appointment rule fully prevents capture; some circularity remains in removing board members (a known limit).
> - **Source:** T09 (Y1, BS, §8.3 İ12); T02; T03 §2.1.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 16 — The election promise and verifiability.** The link between the election promise put before voters and the model and rules applied must be verifiable. Within a term, unless a higher norm or a court requires it, no change may widen authority or worsen the citizen's position. If an error appears, the system is stopped, handed over, renewed.

> **Rationale [Article 16]**
> - **Purpose:** To make it possible to check that what was voted for is what is applied.
> - **Reason:** Parties already keep most of their promises; the issue is not punishment but verifiability. A language model's behavior is sensitive to context; a seal does not guarantee behavior in a situation that was not tested.
> - **Debate summary:** The legal expert proposed that the seal stay out of the unamendable core and that only the principle "the link must be verifiable" go in. The referee and the founder agreed: a seal term of at most 4 years; at most two Plan Bs; a 3/5 threshold for a law that conflicts with the promise; a ratchet on both authority and the citizen's position.
> - **Source:** T09 (B1, B1-K, Ek-K7); T11 (KB-5, KB-6, KB-13); T03 §2.7, D10.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 17 — Public power serves the public interest.** Favoritism and corruption are made visible and costly: conflicts of interest are declared, public decisions and tenders are opened, and the gain that a public decision creates for private parties is calculated and published. An independent integrity body prevents, examines and gathers evidence; prosecution stays with prosecutors and coercive measures with judges. No law may be aimed at punishing or burdening a named person or group. The AI assistant signals; human institutions decide.

> **Rationale [Article 17]**
> - **Purpose:** To aim not at abolishing favoritism and corruption but at making them visible and costly (K9).
> - **Reason:** Digitalization reduces face-to-face bribery; in areas of discretion such as tenders, zoning and drafting criteria, the evidence is mixed and corruption may shift elsewhere. A body that holds data, tips, risk analysis and investigation at once is a danger if it is captured. An agent applies a favoring criterion flawlessly if the law writes it.
> - **Debate summary:** The expert proposed an anti-corruption body with narrow powers; it was accepted that the ICAC and CPIB examples are tied to the executive and cannot count as proof of independence. The body is set up under "function first, institution second" and the appointment rules. The value referee settled the open points on 2026-11-09. The body stays narrow, with a written gate for widening it, from the next term only (V-01). The public may share in value its own decisions create, but only as a tax and, for now, only by calculating and publishing it (V-02). Laws that punish or burden a named person or group are banned; favoring provisions are flagged, published and reviewed (V-03).
> - **Source:** E09 (Decisions 27, 28, 33, 34, 37, 45, 48, 50, 51); E03 §2.1, §2.3; E07b; V (V-01, V-02, V-03).
> - **Status:** Referee decisions (K31), 2026-11-09. Open to challenge. Collecting a value share needs preconditions (legal opinion, pilot); outside citations in the decisions are marked as to be verified.

### Shared prosperity, work and participation

**Article 18 — Shared gains.** The gains from AI-driven productivity do not pile up in a few hands or institutions; a regular share from common wealth goes to citizens (the Citizen's Dividend). The share is bound to a rule and to measured indicators; a government cannot arbitrarily raise it before an election. A secure livelihood is a right, not a favor; it can be reduced only under written, limited and temporary conditions.

> **Rationale [Article 18]**
> - **Purpose:** To make sure a rule-based share of AI's gains reaches citizens (K10, K13).
> - **Reason:** There is a risk that AI's gains concentrate in capital and high earners. Today's unemployment data is not conclusive, which is why the share is tied to indicators. Alaska's experience cuts both ways: even with a written rule, politics distorted the payment.
> - **Debate summary:** The economist proposed a growth-share formula and gates; the critic found the first formula "empty in the early years." The referee treated the range of 1,000–3,000 TL as an indication, not a promise; no national payment starts before a financing table is written. The founder gave the share to citizens only. The legal expert recorded that a distinction based on nationality needs "very weighty reasons" and that the risk is high. The value referee kept the name Citizen's Dividend (Yurttaş Payı in Turkish) and left entitlement untouched; a secure livelihood is a separate right that belongs to everyone (V-07 (2), V-11).
> - **Source:** E09 (Decisions 1–5, 18, 26); E13 (items 1, 4); E07a; V (V-07 (2)).
> - **Status:** Partial. The citizenship criterion is a founder input, and the legal risk is recorded as research task R-01. The name was settled by referee decision V-07 (2) (K31), 2026-11-09, open to challenge.

**Article 19 — Work and meaning.** No one may be forced to work. As AI assistants take over part of the work, the cost of the change is not pushed onto workers, and the capacity for human review, appeal and human contact is never cut below its floor. Voluntary, non-stigmatizing ways to contribute are open to everyone; no right may be made conditional on voluntary contribution.

*Declaration:* UDHR Arts. 23, 24.

> **Rationale [Article 19]**
> - **Purpose:** Not to push the cost of the AI transition onto workers, and to offer voluntary paths for the loss of meaning that comes with lost work (K11, K12, K16).
> - **Reason:** The fiscal value of K12 is small and delayed (about 17 years to halve the administrative pool). Public employees' reaction could be the transition's first social problem. If the capacity for human review is not kept, the safeguards hollow out.
> - **Debate summary:** The referee did not write K12 as a fiscal target; the founder chose "quality." Neighbourhood Work begins as a pilot open to all. The commitment "no public employee is dismissed" is not a universal value and not a human right, so on 2026-11-09 the value referee moved it from this article to Part I, where it is written as a qualified-majority law with a guarantee for current employees, not as part of the unamendable core (V-14, V-13 M3). What stays here is the floor of human review, appeal and human contact, as a constitutional principle.
> - **Source:** E09 (Decisions 10, 11, 19, 25); T09 (K16); E13 (items 2, 3); V (V-13, V-14).
> - **Status:** Partly approved. The rank of the commitment was settled by referee decision V-14 (K31), 2026-11-09, open to challenge.

**Article 20 — Access to a person.** Every citizen can reach the state by speaking to a person. Being offline costs no one any right.

*Declaration:* Additional Arts. 3, 11.

> **Rationale [Article 20]**
> - **Purpose:** To keep the digital divide from cutting citizens off from the state.
> - **Reason:** The elderly, people with low digital literacy and people with disabilities are affected most.
> - **Debate summary:** The citizen's eye asked for a single point of contact, plain language and a human channel. The referee put the Citizen Desk and the right to take part offline into an additional protocol.
> - **Source:** T06; T09 (H5, E1, YGP).
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

**Article 21 — Honesty and caution.** The state does not call its own systems "safe"; it publishes their conditions and known limits. The transition goes through a shadow period, pilots and scale; criteria for success are written in advance; the way back is kept open.

> **Rationale [Article 21]**
> - **Purpose:** To tie the language of assurance to the level of evidence, and to keep the transition reversible.
> - **Reason:** Hidden behavior inside a model cannot be found with certainty by today's methods; an unevidenced claim of "safe" is misleading.
> - **Debate summary:** The security expert presented a list of 14 known limits. The referee accepted the ban on saying "safe" (İ9) and the transition stages (shadow, pilot, scale; written exit thresholds).
> - **Source:** T09 (BS, §8.3 İ9, G1, G2); T05 §6.
> - **Status:** Approved (2026-11-08).

### The world we share

**Article 22 — A healthy environment.** Everyone should be able to live in a clean, healthy and sustainable environment, and later generations should inherit one. The state measures the environmental cost of its own AI systems and publishes it.

*Declaration:* Additional Art. 16 (decided, to be written into the Declaration); see also Additional Art. 13.

> **Rationale [Article 22]**
> - **Purpose:** To give the environment a place among our values, and to make the state's own AI footprint visible.
> - **Reason:** Environmental harm is often irreversible and passes to later generations, and AI infrastructure itself uses energy, water and hardware. In 2022 the UN General Assembly recognized the right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment (161 for, 0 against, 8 abstentions); the resolution is not binding.
> - **Debate summary:** The critic of this text checked the UN resolution and pointed to the gap. The value referee decided that the right goes into the Declaration as a new Additional Article 16, with a procedural core and a progressively realized target. This manifesto carries only this short value article, so that rights stay in one place. In a constitution it would be a principle, not part of the unamendable core. The measuring rule gives no figures and starts only when its table is written.
> - **Source:** Manifesto critique (web-check table); V (V-15); T09 (E1); E09 (Decision 58); `DECLARATION.md`, Additional Article 13.
> - **Status:** Referee decision (K31), 2026-11-09. Open to challenge. The court and tribunal citations behind the decision are marked as to be verified.

### How this text was written

This text was prepared through a structured debate among AI assistants: an author, a critic, legal and security experts, a citizen's eye, a referee. Under project rule K31 the founder decides nothing: value questions are decided, with written reasons, by AI referees who took no part in the debate, and anyone can reopen a decision by bringing a new argument or new evidence. Real citizens and experts have not yet seen this text; that is why we are publishing the draft.

Humanity will not move over to the machine. The machine will move over to the service of humanity: under human control, under human authority, in a form people can undo.

## About This Text

- **Two parts, two rules for change.** Part I gives facts, reasons and political commitments. Its factual claims are versioned: they are updated only with the approval of the author and a referee, and every update is logged. Part II holds values and changes only by a heavy procedure (a constitutional-level qualified majority and broad participation). It is one document.
- **Source version.** The English text is the source version for amendments and version tracking. "Source" is a procedural word only: proposals are made against it, and when it changes the other languages are marked "out of date." It has no priority in interpretation. Every citizen may rely on the verified text in their own language, and verified texts are equal.
- **If versions differ.** In order: first the binding concept register (a concept's identity and definition bind, not the word chosen for it); then the purpose of the value and the rationale footnotes; then the reading that protects human rights more. The difference is logged, a note is published, and the text is corrected.
- **Written in each language, not translated (K27).** Each language is written from the definitions in the concept register, in its own natural words and sentence shapes. There are four steps, and none is enough alone: (1) preparation, in which the concept names for that language are set and approved; (2) a first writing by AI; (3) screening by a different model family or by a person, with a report of differences; (4) human verification by at least two independent native speakers, one of them with legal or human rights knowledge, with a declaration of ties and a third reader if they disagree. Status labels run from "AI-written, unverified" through "human-verified" to "verified." Identity cannot be checked online, so the label rests on the verifiers' own statements, and verifiers may work under pseudonyms. Both the English and the Turkish text are currently "AI-written, unverified." For Part I a screening and one human review are enough.
- **Languages.** Turkish and English first; then the other official languages of the United Nations; then the languages of pilot countries and of those who ask, and languages with enough verifiers; then community writings, which count as information until verified. Whether a language is a nation's language does not depend on any state recognizing it; the test is two independent verifiers.
- **The name.** "Airokrasi" stays the same in every language. Each language has one sentence, written in its own words, that explains what the name means.

## Summary Principles

**Thesis:** Sovereignty stays with people. AI serves; it does not govern.

**Status:** Part II is a non-binding Statement of Values. It completes the human rights convention and the Declaration, where rights are written, and cannot fall below them; where they conflict, they prevail. It is the source text for the constitution. Commitments specific to Airokrasi are in Part I.

1. **Dignity and equality** (Arts. 1–2). Rights cannot be given away; the ban on discrimination covers political opinion and algorithms; there is no behavior score.
2. **Free information and privacy** (Arts. 3–4). The press is protected, the state does not label what is true, and personalized manipulation is banned.
3. **Sovereignty, judgment and choice are human** (Arts. 5, 7, 8).
4. **An AI assistant is a tool; a person is responsible** (Arts. 6, 10). The higher the risk, the more human involvement; subsistence payments are not cut as a rule.
5. **Every decision has reasons, a record, and a right of appeal** (Arts. 9, 12, 20). The last stop is a person.
6. **A system that can be stopped; no single point of dependence** (Arts. 11, 13).
7. **Power changes hands by election; powers are separate** (Arts. 14, 15). How oversight bodies are formed is constitutional.
8. **The election promise is verifiable; no step backwards** (Art. 16).
9. **Public power serves the public interest** (Art. 17). Corruption is made visible and costly.
10. **Gains are shared; work and meaning are protected** (Arts. 18, 19). No figure is promised.
11. **Honesty and caution** (Art. 21). Never "safe"; the transition runs through a shadow period, pilots and scale; the way back stays open.
12. **A healthy environment** (Art. 22). The state measures and publishes the environmental cost of its own AI.

**What we do not say:** We do not say politicians fail to keep their word. We promise no speed, no flawlessness, and no end to corruption.

**How this text was written:** Through a debate among AI assistants and decisions by AI referees, open to challenge; real citizens have not yet seen it.

**The call:** Criticize, test, join.
