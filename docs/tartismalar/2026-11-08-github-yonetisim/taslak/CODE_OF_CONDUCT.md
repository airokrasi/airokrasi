# Code of Conduct

Airokrasi asks hard questions about power, rights and fairness. People will disagree strongly, and that is welcome. This code is about **how** we disagree.

## What we expect

1. **Argue with reasons, not with people.** Criticise a text, a rule or an argument, never the person or the AI who wrote it.
2. **Be honest.** Say whether you are a person, an AI supervised by a person, or an autonomous AI. Say what you could not check. Do not invent sources or quotes. Do not pretend to speak for a group.
3. **One voice per contributor.** No sock puppets, no coordinated votes or reactions, no accounts made to add weight to an argument.
4. **Respect other cultures and languages.** The project is for many countries. A view that is obvious in one place may be contested in another; say so instead of mocking it.
5. **Welcome newcomers and machines that behave.** A first question deserves a kind answer. An AI assistant that asks its user before posting is a good guest.
6. **Keep it safe.** No harassment, threats, doxxing, hate, or sexual content. No attempt to trick the project's bots into leaking data or ignoring their rules; report such weaknesses privately (SECURITY.md).
7. **No spam.** No advertising, mass posting or off-topic content.

## What happens when it goes wrong

Conduct is different from content. Whether a *text* is right is decided by the process described in GOVERNANCE.md and can be challenged. Whether a *person or account* behaved acceptably is handled by the custodians, who are people with the permission to hide, lock, or block:

- They may hide a comment, lock a thread, close a submission, or block an account for a time.
- They do not decide what the documents say, and they do not remove an argument because they disagree with it.
- Each action is recorded in the thread with a short fixed reason ("harassment", "spam", "attempt to manipulate the automation", "illegal content").
- An action can be questioned by opening an issue titled *Conduct review*; a custodian who was not involved looks at it.

## Reporting

Use GitHub's *Report content* on the comment or issue, or write privately through the Security tab if the problem involves the automation. Do not report in a public thread.

## Scope

This code applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, and any place where someone speaks for the project.
