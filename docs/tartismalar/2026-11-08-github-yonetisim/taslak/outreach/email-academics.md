# E-mail to academics: drafts

Send one personal e-mail per person, from your own address and under your real name. Write 2 or 3 sentences about why *their* work is relevant (do not skip this: a generic message is spam). Offer an easy no. Do not use mail merge to people you do not know. Check the anti-spam rules that apply to the recipient.

## English
Subject: A request for your critique: an open project on AI agents and democratic legitimacy

Dear Professor [Name],

I am involved in Airokrasi, an open project that explores a governance model in which AI agents carry out day-to-day legislative, executive and judicial work while sovereignty and the last word stay with people. I am writing because [one specific sentence about their work, for example "your work on algorithmic accountability in public administration"].

It is a set of public documents: a manifesto, a declaration of rights built on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, and recorded debates between AI roles. The repository runs a small copy of the idea. AI roles decide with written reasons, nobody approves by hand, and anyone can challenge a decision with a new argument or new evidence. We state the weaknesses openly, above all that machine-made decisions have no democratic legitimacy by themselves.

What would help most is your strongest objection, a pointer to literature we have missed, or a country-level view. You can write it as an issue at https://github.com/airokrasi/airokrasi (CONTRIBUTING.md explains how), or just reply to me. If this is not of interest, no reply is needed and I will not write again.

With thanks,
[Your name, role, affiliation if any]

Airokrasi'nin içinde yer alıyorum. Bu, yapay zekâ ajanlarının günlük yasama, yürütme ve yargı işlerini yürüttüğü, egemenliğin ve son sözün insanda kaldığı bir yönetim modelini inceleyen açık bir proje. Size yazma nedenim: [çalışmalarıyla ilgili tek ve somut bir cümle].

Saygılarımla,
